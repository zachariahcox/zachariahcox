## Looking back

### 1\. Accomplishments

* Summarize your top 3 to 5 accomplishments for the period.
* Focus on personal impact, not activities, not how cool the product was.
* Consider using the three circles of impact.
* URLs are good, but don't link to all your PRs.
* For managers, include management and team accomplishments


### 2\. What could have gone better? 

* Apply a growth mindset
* Use the [CARL](https://reflection.ed.ac.uk/reflectors-toolkit/reflecting-on-experience/carl) framework to describe two or three situations where you could have had more impact.   
* What happened? What would you do differently next time? Be specific.

# Peer feedback questions

Nominate 3-to-5 other employees to be asked for feedback.
Give those nominees at least a few days to respond.

* What is your name, role and relation to this person?
* Describe a situation where this person did something really well.
* Describe a situation where this person could have done something better.
* Anything else?

## How to write peer feedback so managers will listen 😂

1. Keep it short -- this lets more of your writing make it through unaltered and saves everyone time.
2. Describe specific situations and let the reader come to their own conclusions. There's no need to assess the career progression of a peer (that's their manager's job!).
3. Do not use words like "sometimes," "always," "never," etc. (see point 2!)
5. Think about how this feedback would be anonymized, aggregated, and delivered. If you were the manager reading this, how would _you_ summarize it into one sentence?
6. If the "what could have gone better" question seems hard to answer, reframe it in your head to "what should this person focus on to be promoted to the next level?"

# Principles

### No surprises

If anything written here from either side astonishes the other, the first action item is to adjust how we run our 1:1s to prevent this in the future.

Sometimes, it is only through deep reflection (or the extra data provided by peer feedback) that certain patterns are identified. 
These are quite interesting surprises, but adjusting 1:1s to avoid them is still the right move.

### Focus on themes when aggregating peer feedback

I only include _themes_ from peer feedback, meaning that it came up in at least two responses.
If only one peer has a specific piece of feedback, pay attention but know that it could represent its own kind of bias.

### Consider the audience

For the most part, no one besides the manager and employee will ever read these. 
With such shared context, it's often sufficient to use short formats like: 

* Bullet point
  * Justification 1 (link)
  * Justification 2 (link)
 
The links are there for those future managers to review and also to ensure there are no misunderstandings.
